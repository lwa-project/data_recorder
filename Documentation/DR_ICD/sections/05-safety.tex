\chapter{Safety Interface}
\label{sec:safety}

The DR is primarily a software system running on standard PC hardware and does
not present significant physical safety hazards beyond those associated with
normal computing equipment.  The following monitoring and protection mechanisms
are provided.

\section{Temperature Monitoring}

CPU core temperatures are monitored via the \mib{CPU-COUNT} and
\mib{CPU-TEMP-\{n\}} MIB entries (Section~\ref{mib:cpu-count}).  Hard drive
temperatures are monitored via the \mib{HDD-COUNT} and \mib{HDD-TEMP-\{n\}} MIB
entries (Section~\ref{mib:hdd-count}).  MCS or an external monitoring system
may poll these entries to detect overheating conditions.

Temperature monitoring depends on hardware support.  Some hardware may not
report HDD temperatures; in such cases, the \mib{HDD-TEMP-\{n\}} response may be
empty.

\section{Storage Monitoring}

Available storage space is tracked through the \mib{TOTAL-STORAGE} and
\mib{REMAINING-STORAGE} MIB entries (Sections~\ref{mib:total-storage}
and~\ref{mib:remaining-storage}).  The \cmd{REC} command checks for
sufficient free space before scheduling a recording and rejects the request
with an ``Insufficient Drive Space'' error if storage is insufficient.

Per-device storage is also available through the \mib{DEVICE-COUNT} and
\mib{DEVICE-STORAGE-\{n\}} MIB entries for external devices.

\section{Error Recovery}

If the DR enters an error state, the \cmd{INI} command
(Section~\ref{cmd:ini}) can be used to re-initialize the system without
requiring a full reboot.  The \texttt{-{}-flush-log} and
\texttt{-{}-flush-data} flags provide additional control over what is reset
during re-initialization.

When deployed as a \texttt{systemd} service, DROS2 is configured to restart
automatically after an unexpected exit, with a 60-second delay between restart
attempts.

\section{Power}
Standard computer power safety practices apply. No special power conditioning
or UPS is required by the DR itself, although site-level power protection is
recommended to avoid data loss from unexpected power failure during recording
operations.
